# Getting Fully Booked — The £2,000/Week Plan

**Prepared:** 2 August 2026
**Target:** £2,000/week revenue (~£400 per working day)
**Constraint:** Josh works alone.

**Research basis:** Meta Ad Library (3,139 active UK "car detailing" ads; 192 active "ceramic coating / paint correction" ads), web search of bluehorizondetailing.com and its reviews, and Blue Horizon's own campaign data from the last week. Direct page loads are blocked by this environment's network policy, so website findings come from indexed search results, not a rendered page.

# 1. The uncomfortable maths — read this first

£2,000/week solo means roughly **£400 per working day**. The only question that matters is: *what does each service earn per day of Josh's time?*

| Service | Price | Realistic time | **Revenue per working day** |
|---|---|---|---|
| Maintenance Valet | £80 | ~0.4 day | **£200** ❌ |
| Maintenance Plus | £110 | ~0.5 day | **£220** ❌ |
| **Deep Clean at £150 offer** | £150 | ~0.7 day | **£214** ❌ |
| Deep Clean at list | £180 | ~0.7 day | **£257** ❌ |
| Flawless Finish | £745 | ~2 days | **£373** ⚠️ |
| Refined Correction | £595 | ~1.5 days | **£397** ⚠️ |
| **SMART repair** | £150 | ~0.35 day | **£430** ✅ |
| **New Car Protection** | £675 | ~1.5 days | **£450** ✅ |
| **Enhancement Detail** | £495 | ~1 day | **£495** ✅ |

## The single most important conclusion

**No amount of Deep Cleans gets to £2,000/week.** At £150 they yield ~£214 per working day. Even at full £180 list price it's £257. Working six days flat out at £150 a clean tops out around **£1,300/week** — and that's with zero gaps, zero travel waste and no admin.

**The £150 Deep Clean offer is not a growth engine. It is a customer-acquisition cost.** That's fine — if it converts into repeat and higher-ticket work. It is not fine as the main thing being advertised, which is what it has been.

To hit £2,000/week the **average job value has to be around £300**, against a current headline offer of £150.

From 3,139 active UK detailing ads, the patterns are consistent and instructive.

| Advertiser | Advertised |

| Kadz Detailing | **GTechniq 5 Year Ceramic Coating Offer** |
| **Blue Horizon** | **Flawless Finish £745 · New Car Protection £675** |

Josh's packages include full multi-stage correction, so they aren't like-for-like with a £399 coating-only job. **But the customer scrolling Facebook doesn't know that.** Either the ad makes the difference explicit in the first two lines, or there needs to be an entry-level ceramic tier to compete at the price point people are being trained to expect.

### Six tactics almost everyone uses that Blue Horizon currently doesn't

1. **Price anchoring on high-ticket.** "Regular £900, now £445." Blue Horizon does this correctly on the Deep Clean ("Usually £180, currently £150") but not on any premium service.
2. **Review count as the headline.** Kadz runs *"110+ Five-Star Google Reviews. See Why."* as a primary headline. Blue Horizon has **50+ reviews at 4.8** and buries it in a description line. This is the most under-used asset in the account.
3. **Durability claims.** "3-Year Protection", "5 Year Ceramic". Blue Horizon claims none — correctly, since the knowledge file forbids unsupported durability claims. **But this is a real competitive disadvantage.** → *Action: confirm what Gtechniq CSL's actual stated durability is. If the product supports a figure, Josh can and should state it. If not, compete on "protection you can feel, applied properly" instead.*
4. **Dynamic creative with 5–6 headline variants** per ad (Kadz, Hadrian's). Blue Horizon runs one headline per ad.
5. **Instant lead forms.** Noir Detailing: *"Complete the quick form below to receive a personalised quote."* Worth testing against WhatsApp — different people convert on different mechanisms.
6. **Seasonal hooks.** "Transform Your Car This Summer", "Save £200 This June."

### The genuine opportunity

**3,139 active ads for "car detailing" — but only 192 for "ceramic coating / paint correction."**

The cheap end is a bloodbath. The high-ticket end has roughly **6% of the competition** — and it's where the £400/day work is. That is the gap to attack.

# 5. The plan

## Principle
**Stop advertising the cheapest thing you sell.** Every hour spent on a £150 Deep Clean is an hour not spent on a £675 job. The Deep Clean's job is to *acquire* customers, not to fill the diary.

## Layer 1 — Recurring base (the real answer to "fully booked every week")

This is the part that makes weeks predictable rather than a permanent acquisition treadmill.

**Target: 35–40 maintenance customers on 4–8 week cycles.**

At 35 customers averaging £95 on a 6-week rotation, that's ~6 jobs/week and **~£550/week of guaranteed revenue with zero ad spend** — roughly 28% of target, locked in, filling the gaps between big jobs.

**How to build it:** every Deep Clean customer gets offered the maintenance slot at handover, while they're looking at the result. That conversation costs nothing and is the highest-conversion sales moment in the business.

## Layer 2 — High-ticket acquisition (the revenue engine)

**Meta:** lead with **New Car Protection (£675)** and **Enhancement Detail (£495)**. Both clear £450–495/day. Only 192 competing ads nationally.

**Google Search:** this is where high-intent buyers are, and the knowledge file already says so. Someone searching *"ceramic coating rugby"* is ready to buy in a way no Facebook scroller is. £10–15/day here will likely outperform the same money on Meta for high-ticket work.

## Layer 3 — Gap filler

**SMART repairs at £430/day** are the best-kept secret in the catalogue: quick turnaround, high hourly yield, and they slot into half-days that would otherwise be wasted. Door scratch was already the top SMART performer in the ads.

## Layer 4 — Upsell (free revenue)

Every Deep Clean on a decent car is a live correction/ceramic prospect. A Range Rover getting a £150 clean is a £675 New Car Protection conversation. **This costs £0 in ad spend and is almost certainly worth more this month than any new campaign.**

# 7. Honest assessment

**£2,000/week is achievable, but it is close to full solo capacity.** It works out at roughly **£45–57 per billable hour** after travel and admin. That's a genuine premium rate — attainable on detailing and SMART work, and simply not attainable on valeting.

**What has to be true:**
- Average job value moves from ~£150 to ~£300
- At least one £495+ job books every week
- Diary utilisation stays high — gaps are what kill the number
- Response speed stays fast (still the biggest single driver of bookings)

**What would make it fail:**
- Continuing to lead advertising with the cheapest service
- No recurring base, so every week restarts from zero
- Ad spend chasing volume instead of value

**The honest risk:** at full capacity there is no headroom. Illness, weather or a van problem costs a full week. Getting past ~£2,500/week means a second pair of hands — worth planning for before it's urgent, not after.
